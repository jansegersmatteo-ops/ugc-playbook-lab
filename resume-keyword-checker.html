How to use the result

  • Check the hard requirements first. A licence, the right to work in the country, a location or shift: if the ad lists one you do not meet, a keyword will not fix it.
  • Use the ad's exact words where they are true. If you did "customer onboarding" but your resume calls it "new client setup", use the ad's term. A recruiter scanning for it, by eye or with a search, finds it faster.
  • Add each term once, inside a result. "Cut customer onboarding time from 10 to 6 days by rewriting the welcome emails" beats a skills list stuffed with every keyword.
  • Ignore terms that are not about the job. The checker is simple: it can pick up company names or generic words. Skip those.

Do ATS systems really reject resumes automatically?

Mostly not for the reasons you hear online. The often-quoted claim that "75% of resumes are rejected by ATS" has no published study behind it; it is traced to a 2012 sales pitch by Preptel, a resume-optimisation company that closed in 2013.

What can reject you automatically is documented by the ATS vendors. Greenhouse, for example, lets employers auto-reject candidates based on their answers to application questions (on its Plus and Pro plans) and block repeat applications within a set number of days. Your font or a keyword count is not on that list.

In a 2025 survey by the resume company Enhancv, 92% of 25 US recruiters said their ATS does not automatically reject resumes for formatting, design, missing keywords or a low match score. The survey is small and comes from a company that sells resume help, but it matches the vendor documentation. Recruiters said clear, skimmable structure (92%), relevant experience (88%) and tailoring without keyword stuffing (76%) help a resume advance.

Does tailoring pay off?

The job tracker Huntr compared its users' applications from October 2024 to June 2026: tailored resumes were marked "interviewed" in 5.71% of applications, untailored ones in 3.09%. Huntr notes these are descriptive figures from its own users, not proof of cause and effect, so read them as a signal, not a promise.
