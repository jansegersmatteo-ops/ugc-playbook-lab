How to become a UGC creator with no followers

A UGC creator makes short, natural-looking videos that brands use in ads and on their own pages. Unlike an influencer, you sell the video, not access to an audience. That's why you can start with zero followers.

Step 1: pick a niche you can film at home

Choose 1–2 niches based on what you already own and use: kitchen gadgets, skincare, fitness gear, apps and desk setups, pet products. Brands want creators who look like their customers.

Step 2: set up with what you have

  • Any recent smartphone, filmed vertically (9:16).
  • Daylight from a window, facing you.
  • A quiet room with soft furnishings for clean sound.
  • A stack of books as a tripod.

Buy nothing until a client pays you.

Step 3: learn the structure of a UGC ad

Hook (first 3 seconds) → problem → product as solution → one concrete detail → call to action. Use an AI assistant to draft scripts and hooks, then rewrite every line until it sounds like you.

Prompt: You are an experienced UGC scriptwriter. Write a 30-second UGC ad script for [product] for [customer]: a hook in the first 3 seconds, the problem, how the product solves it, one detail only a real user would notice, and a call to action. Natural spoken language, no hype words. Then give me 5 alternative hooks.

Step 4: build a portfolio of 3 spec videos

A spec video is a sample you make with a product you bought or own. Make three different formats (problem–solution ad, demo, honest review), label them clearly as samples, and put them on one simple portfolio page.

Step 5: set your rates

Most posted per-video offers in 2026 sat between $50 and $200 (see our UGC rates guide). Price per deliverable and charge extra for paid-ad usage.

Step 6: find brands and pitch

Brands already running creator-style ads already buy UGC. You can see who's advertising in public ad libraries. Send short, personal pitches with one specific video idea (see our pitch templates), and apply on one UGC marketplace for your first jobs.

An honest note

UGC is real work. Some creators land deals in weeks, others take months. Consistency matters more than talent: a few pitches every week, a new spec video every month, and better rates as your portfolio grows.
