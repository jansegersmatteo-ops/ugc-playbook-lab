How to write a UGC script (template + examples)

A good UGC script sounds like a real person talking, but it is built like an ad. Brands judge you on two things: do people keep watching, and do they understand the product. This guide gives you one template, word counts per length, three example scripts and a prompt to draft faster.

Why the first seconds matter most

  • TikTok says over 63% of the auction ads with the highest click-through rates showed the key message or product within the first 3 seconds.
  • TikTok's own research found that 90% of ad recall impact is captured in the first six seconds.
  • On Meta, Billo measured an average hook rate of 25.44% across 88,329 sales-objective video ads in H1 2026 (3-second plays divided by impressions). Around 30% or more is above every industry average.

In practice: your first line and first shot carry most of the weight. Write them last, and write five versions.

The template: 5 parts

PartJobTime (30-sec video)
1. HookStop the scroll: a question, a bold statement or a visual surprise0–3 sec
2. ProblemName a frustration the viewer recognises3–8 sec
3. SolutionShow the product solving it, on screen8–20 sec
4. Proof detailOne concrete detail only a real user would notice20–25 sec
5. Call to actionTell the viewer the next step, short and clear25–30 sec

Show the product on screen early: TikTok reports that ads showing the product were associated with a 65% increase in brand affinity. A CTA card was associated with a 45% lift in recall.

How many words per length?

Billo puts a 30-second script at 75–120 words. That is about 2.5–4 words per second, so as a rough guide:

  • 15 seconds: 35–60 words. Hook, product, one benefit, CTA.
  • 30 seconds: 75–120 words. The full 5-part template.
  • 60 seconds: 150–220 words. Add a demo step or a short story.

30 seconds is the length brands order most on Billo: 60% of approved videos in the past 12 months, against 25% for 60 seconds and 13% for 15 seconds.

Example 1: problem–solution (30 sec, skincare)

Hook: "I stopped buying a new moisturiser every month. Here's why."
Problem: "My skin was tight by lunch, no matter what I used."
Solution: "This one is a gel-cream. Two pumps, it sinks in in about a minute, no shine."
Proof: "The pump stops at exactly one dose, so I'm not wasting half the bottle."
CTA: "If your skin feels tight by noon, try it. Link's below."

Example 2: demo (30 sec, kitchen gadget)

Hook: (on screen, cutting) "Watch how long this takes."
Problem: "Dicing onions used to take me five minutes and a lot of tears."
Solution: "Half an onion, press twice, done. The blades lift out for the dishwasher."
Proof: "The base has a rubber ring, so it doesn't slide on the counter."
CTA: "Save this for your next meal prep."

Example 3: honest review (15 sec, app)

Hook: "One week using this budgeting app. Honest take."
Body: "Good: it sorts my card spending automatically. Not great: the free version only tracks two accounts."
CTA: "If you only need the basics, start with the free version."

An honest "not great" line often makes a review more believable. Agree it with the brand first.

Five hook formats to rotate

  1. Question: "Why does nobody talk about...?"
  2. Result first: "This is my desk after one weekend."
  3. Mistake: "I was using this wrong for a year."
  4. Contrast: "$12 vs $60: can you tell the difference?"
  5. Visual: start mid-action, product already in hand.

An AI prompt to draft faster

You are an experienced UGC scriptwriter. Product: [product]. Customer: [who buys it]. Main problem it solves: [problem]. Write a 30-second UGC script of 75–120 words in natural spoken English with these parts labelled: hook (max 10 words), problem, solution with what we see on screen, one proof detail a real user would notice, call to action. No hype words, no claims I can't verify. Then write 5 alternative hooks using these formats: question, result first, mistake, contrast, visual.

Then rewrite every line until it sounds like you, and check every claim against the brand brief. Never invent results, prices or reviews.

Checklist before you film

  • Hook is under 10 words and fits in 3 seconds
  • Product is on screen in the first seconds
  • Word count fits the length
  • Only claims the brand approved
  • One clear CTA
  • Read it out loud: does it sound like you?
