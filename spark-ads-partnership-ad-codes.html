Spark Ads and partnership ad codes: what to send a brand

Sooner or later a brand asks: "Can you send us the Spark code?" or "Can you share a partnership ad code?" Both mean the same thing. The brand wants to run your post as a paid ad, shown from your handle. This is often called whitelisting. Here is how to create the code on each platform, which settings matter, and what to agree on first.

Delivering a file vs. sending a code

What the brand asks forWhose handle the ad showsWhat you give
Paid ads usageThe brand's own accountThe video file plus usage rights in writing
TikTok Spark AdsYour TikTok accountA video code for a post on your account
Meta partnership adsYour handle together with the brand'sA partnership ad code for a post on your account

Meta describes a partnership ad as content a brand posts in collaboration with a creator, featuring the creator's handle. TikTok lets advertisers run Spark Ads from creators' posts that were authorized with a video code.

TikTok: how to generate a Spark Ads code

These steps follow TikTok's own Spark Ads guide for advertisers. Menu names can differ slightly between app versions.

  1. Turn on ad settings for your account. Profile → three dots (top right) → Creator tools → switch on Ad settings. In some app versions this sits under Settings and privacy → Privacy.
  2. Authorize the video. Open the video → three dots → Ad settings → agree to the Advertising Terms of Service → turn on Ad authorization.
  3. Generate the code. Tap Generate code, choose an authorization period (7, 30, 60 or 365 days; 365 days needs app version 22.7 or later), tap Copy code and send it to the brand.

Good to know before you send it (from TikTok's help pages):

  • You cannot edit the caption after the post is authorized as an ad.
  • A private video becomes public once it is used in a campaign, and you cannot change its privacy while it is promoted.
  • You can extend the period from the post's Ad settings, but only before the code expires.
  • Do not delete the video while an ad is running on it. Billo, a UGC platform, tells its creators the same.

Instagram: how to get a partnership ad code

  1. Use a professional account (Creator or Business) with branded content tools on. If you do not see the option: Settings → For professionals → Creator tools and controls → Branded content.
  2. Open the post, Reel or story → three dots → Partnership label and ads.
  3. Turn on Get partnership ad code, copy the code and send it to the brand. You can also switch it on while creating a new post.

Facebook: open the published content → three dots → Share partnership ad code → turn on Get partnership ad code. The content must stay published; archived Facebook content cannot be used.

How long does an Instagram code last? Sources disagree. Billo's creator help says a code is valid for 60 days unless you revoke it. Leadsie's help says partners can use it as long as the toggle stays on. Meta's own creator blog does not give a fixed period, and says brands set the ad's budget and duration. So do not rely on expiry: agree the period in writing and revoke the code yourself when it ends (three dots → turn the code off, or check active partnership ads in your creator settings).

Meta also notes that ad results sit in the brand's account. Ask the brand to share them; they make a strong case study for your next pitch.

Price the period, not just the video

A code lets a brand spend money behind your face and handle, so it is a separate usage right. Billo lists whitelisting/Spark Ads as an add-on of roughly 25–50% of the base fee per 30 days (see UGC usage rights explained). Match the code's period to what you sold: on TikTok, 30 days of usage means a 30-day code, not 365. Some platforms set a fixed period; Billo, for example, requires 30 days.

Checklist before you send a code

  • Which post, and which platform?
  • For how long (and does the TikTok period match the contract)?
  • What is the fee for this period, and for an extension?
  • Paid partnership label on (Instagram) and correct ad disclosure?
  • Will the brand share ad results with you?
  • A reminder in your calendar for the end date, so you can revoke or renew.

This is general information, not legal advice. Platform menus and rules change; check the platform's help pages if a step looks different.
